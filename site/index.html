Reading the contract…

A prize for the first proof of 50,000,000 BIT in unspent Bitcraft items

A contract on Sepolia pays its whole balance to the first zero-knowledge proof that someone holds unspent Bitcraft items worth at least 50,000,000 BIT, counted as the Bitcraft app counts them, at a state of the game posted in the last 24 hours. The proof names who gets paid. It doesn't show which items, or how many.

    The terms

    How to claim

    You prove on your own machine, from your item files. You need git, Rust (nightly), Go 1.24, about 2 GB of disk for the proving key and 10 GB of memory for the last step. The first time takes about half an hour, most of it building; later claims take minutes.

    1. Build the prover and the wrapper

      Once. holdings builds inside the game's own workspace, at the release the network runs, so it hashes items as the network does.

    2. Download the Groth16 setup

      Once, 1.7 GB. The contract's verifier was generated from this setup, so a claim needs its proving key. The sums are also in the repository.

    3. Pick a state and stop playing

      Your item files must hold your items as they were at the state's block. Pick a root whose block came after your last action in the game, and don't play again until you've proved. Roots are posted hourly, about 16 minutes behind the chain. Choosing one in the column above fills in its block.

    4. Dry run

      Counts your live items at that state with pod2's mock prover and picks the fewest, most valuable ones that reach the threshold. It prints the claim by class, or how far short you are.

    5. Prove

      The same, for real. On an M3 Pro, 61 items took 264 s and 4 GB. Your files stay on your machine; the synchronizer is asked for each item's proof of creation and of not being spent.

    6. Wrap the proof for Ethereum

      The wrapper takes about 15 s. The Groth16 proof takes about 2 minutes after loading the proving key, and 10 GB of memory. It writes groth/solidity_calldata.json.

    7. Check it and send it

      Paste groth/solidity_calldata.json below, before the root's 24 hours are up.

    Check and send your claim

    The page reads the claim as the contract does, then asks a Sepolia node whether it would pay. Nothing is sent until you press Send.

      Or send it with Foundry. Anyone may send it: the proof decides who is paid.

      What a claim shows

      Public

      • the address the prize goes to;
      • the state root the items were held at;
      • that their values add up to at least the threshold;
      • the predicate proved, which fixes every class's value.

      Private

      • which items, how many, and what each is worth;
      • their nullifiers, so no one can watch for them being spent;
      • their tags, the IDs that stay the same across an item's states.

      The synchronizer you prove against learns which items you asked about. Prove against your own (--sync URL) to keep that private; the synchronizer folder in the repository shows how to run one.

      What you have to trust

      This is a test-token bounty. Three things keep it from being one for real money:

      • The Groth16 setup is one machine's. Whoever ran it could forge proofs for this verifier. A bounty for real money needs a multi-party setup, or a proof system with a universal one.
      • The root poster is trusted. A made-up state root can commit to made-up items, so the poster's key is worth the prize. The poster takes each root from a synchronizer that checks every transaction's proof itself and compares it with the game's hosted one before posting; this page compares the newest root with both too. The contract can't check either.
      • A claim is about one posted state, not now. Items held at that state can be spent afterwards and the claim still pays.
